Key Highlights:
- 100% Free Care: The hospital's core mission is to provide free healthcare to every child, regardless of their family's financial status, preventing families from falling into debt due to medical bills.
- The "Beatocello" Legacy: For decades, Dr. Beat Richner performed cello concerts every Saturday evening to raise funds. Although he passed away in 2018, his legacy continues, and the hospital remains funded largely by private donations and a $2 contribution from every Angkor Wat ticket sold.
- Jayavarman VII Maternity Ward: This specific branch includes a massive maternity wing designed to reduce maternal mortality and prevent Mother-to-Child transmission of HIV/AIDS, delivering tens of thousands of babies annually.
- High Medical Standards: Despite being a charity, the hospital maintains First World medical standards, using modern equipment and medicines that are often superior to those found in paid private clinics.
- Blood Donation Center: It serves as a critical blood bank for the province. Because of its high volume of surgeries and Dengue fever cases, it is one of the most reliable and safe places for tourists to donate blood.

Visitor Information:
- Location: Vithei Charles de Gaulle (Road to Angkor Wat), Siem Reap. (It is located right next to the Angkor National Museum).
- Getting There: It is on the main road heading north from the city center. A tuk-tuk ride takes about 5 minutes from the Old Market ($1–$2).
- Best Time to Visit: If you wish to donate blood, go between 08:00 AM – 11:00 AM or 02:00 PM – 04:00 PM on weekdays.
- Opening Hours:
- Emergency/Patient Care: 24 Hours / 7 Days a week.
- Blood Donation: Mon – Sun: 08:00 AM – 11:00 AM / 02:00 PM – 04:00 PM (Hours may vary slightly on weekends).
- Information Center: Evenings (Check local schedule for the documentary film screenings, usually Saturdays at 7:15 PM).
- Entrance Fee: Free. Donations are voluntarily accepted at the information center.
- Facilities: A dedicated information hall (where the concerts used to be held), blood donation room, and large waiting gardens for families.
- Accessibility: Fully wheelchair accessible.
- Tips for Visiting Kantha Bopha Hospital:
- Strict "No Tourist" Policy in Wards: You cannot wander through the patient wards to "look" at sick children. This is a working hospital, not a zoo. Respect patient privacy strictly.
- Donate Blood: This is the best way to visit. The process is sterile, professional, and takes about 45 minutes. You will receive a T-shirt and a snack, and you will be directly saving a life (often a child with Dengue or needing surgery).
- Watch the Movie: Since Dr. Beat Richner's passing, the hospital often screens a documentary film about his work and the hospital's history on Saturday evenings. It is a moving experience.
- Dress Modestly: If you enter the compound to donate or inquire, dress as you would for a temple (shoulders and knees covered) out of respect for the local staff and patients.
- Cash Donations: If you cannot give blood, you can make a cash donation at the official office near the entrance. They are rigorously transparent with funds.

Why Visit:
- To perform a life-saving act by donating blood in a safe, international-standard environment.
- To support an organization that treats 85% of Cambodia's seriously ill children.
- To pay respects to the legacy of Dr. Beat Richner, a national hero in Cambodia.
- To learn about the reality of public health challenges in developing nations.
- To contribute financially to a cause where you know 100% of the money goes to patient care.
